Strap Care & Fit

A strap takes all the weight and most of the wear. Fit it well and look after it, and the whole bag lasts longer.

IWidth and comfort

Narrow straps look elegant but concentrate weight on a small area of the shoulder. For everyday bags that carry more, a strap of three to five centimetres feels noticeably lighter and stays put.

IIFinding your length

For a crossbody, the bag should sit around hip height on the opposite side. For a shoulder bag, it should tuck under the arm without swinging. Most straps adjust in steps; if not, a leather repairer can add holes.

IIICaring for leather straps

  • Wipe with a soft, dry cloth after use.
  • Condition a few times a year, testing first.
  • Keep away from heat and direct sun.
  • Let a twisted strap rest flat overnight.

IVChains and hardware

Wipe chains with a dry cloth to remove skin oils, avoid perfume and hand cream, and store the chain inside the bag so it does not scratch the leather. Check that clips and swivel hooks close firmly.

VWhen to repair

Fraying edges, cracking at fold points, or stretched holes are all signs a strap needs attention. Edges can be repainted, holes reinforced and whole straps replaced, often for far less than a new bag.
